The 2×2 formula does not generalize conveniently. Row reduction does, and it also detects when no inverse exists.

<Definition term="The [A | I] method">

To invert an $n \times n$ matrix $A$:

1. Form the $n \times 2n$ matrix $[A \mid I_n]$.
2. Row reduce it with Gauss–Jordan elimination.
3. If the left half becomes $I_n$, the right half is $A^{-1}$:
$$[A \mid I] \;\longrightarrow\; [I \mid A^{-1}].$$
4. If the left half cannot become $I$ (a column without a pivot, often visible as a row of zeros on the left), $A$ is not invertible.

</Definition>

**Why it works.** Every row operation is the same as multiplying on the left by a fixed invertible matrix. If a sequence of them turns $A$ into $I$, their combined effect is a matrix $E$ with $EA = I$, so $E = A^{-1}$. Applying the same operations to $I$ produces $EI = E = A^{-1}$ on the right.

<Example title="A = [1 2; 3 4]">

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left[\begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 4 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right]
\xrightarrow{R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 3R_1}
\left[\begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 & -3 & 1 \end{array}\right] \\[6pt]
&\xrightarrow{R_2 \leftarrow -\frac12 R_2}
\left[\begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac32 & -\frac12 \end{array}\right]
\xrightarrow{R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 2R_2}
\left[\begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 0 & -2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac32 & -\frac12 \end{array}\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

So $A^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 1 \\ \frac32 & -\frac12 \end{bmatrix}$, matching the 2×2 formula from the previous lesson.

</Example>

## A 3×3 inverse, step by step

For $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 5 & 6 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ the reduction takes five steps (every pivot it meets is already 1) and ends with

$$
A^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} -24 & 18 & 5 \\ 20 & -15 & -4 \\ -5 & 4 & 1 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

## When there is no inverse

<Example title="A = [1 2; 2 4]">

$$
\left[\begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 4 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right]
\xrightarrow{R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 2R_1}
\left[\begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 & 1 \end{array}\right]
$$

The left half now has a row of zeros, so column 2 can never receive a pivot and the left half can never become $I$. $A$ is singular.

</Example>
