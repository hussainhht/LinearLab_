**Gaussian elimination** row reduces a matrix to row echelon form; the solution is then found by back substitution. **Gauss–Jordan elimination** keeps going to reduced row echelon form, where the solution can be read off directly. LinearLab's solver uses Gauss–Jordan elimination.

## The algorithm

Work through the columns from left to right, keeping track of the row where the next pivot belongs (it starts at row 1). For each column:

1. **Find a pivot.** Look at this column from the pivot row down. If every entry is 0, the column has no pivot; move to the next column. Otherwise, if the pivot position holds 0, swap in a row below that has a nonzero entry.
2. **Make it 1.** Scale the pivot row by $1/\text{pivot}$.
3. **Clear the column.** For every other row with a nonzero entry $c$ in this column, apply $R_i \leftarrow R_i - cR_{\text{pivot}}$.
4. Move the pivot row down by one and continue with the next column.

When the columns run out, the matrix is in reduced row echelon form.

<Example title="x + 2y = 5 and 3x − y = 4">

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 2 & 5 \\ 3 & -1 & 4 \end{array}\right]
\xrightarrow{R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 3R_1}
\left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 2 & 5 \\ 0 & -7 & -11 \end{array}\right] \\[6pt]
&\xrightarrow{R_2 \leftarrow -\frac17 R_2}
\left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 2 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{11}{7} \end{array}\right]
\xrightarrow{R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 2R_2}
\left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 0 & \frac{13}{7} \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{11}{7} \end{array}\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

The last matrix reads $x = \frac{13}{7}$ and $y = \frac{11}{7}$.

**Check by substitution:** $\frac{13}{7} + 2 \cdot \frac{11}{7} = \frac{35}{7} = 5$ and $3 \cdot \frac{13}{7} - \frac{11}{7} = \frac{28}{7} = 4$. Both equations hold.

</Example>
