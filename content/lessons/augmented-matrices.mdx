Writing $x_1, x_2, \dots$ and plus signs over and over adds nothing: a system is determined by its numbers alone. We store those numbers in two matrices.

The **coefficient matrix** $A$ holds the coefficients, one row per equation and one column per variable. The **augmented matrix** $[A \mid b]$ adds the constants as a final column, separated by a vertical bar:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix}
a_{11} & a_{12} & \cdots & a_{1p} \\
a_{21} & a_{22} & \cdots & a_{2p} \\
\vdots & \vdots & & \vdots \\
a_{n1} & a_{n2} & \cdots & a_{np}
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
[A \mid b] = \left[\begin{array}{cccc|c}
a_{11} & a_{12} & \cdots & a_{1p} & b_1 \\
a_{21} & a_{22} & \cdots & a_{2p} & b_2 \\
\vdots & \vdots & & \vdots & \vdots \\
a_{n1} & a_{n2} & \cdots & a_{np} & b_n
\end{array}\right]
$$

The bar is only a reminder of where the equals signs were. Two rules make the translation reliable: keep the variables in the same order in every row, and write $0$ for a variable that is missing from an equation.

<Example title="From a system to its augmented matrix">

$$
\begin{aligned} 2x - 3y + 4z &= 1 \\ x - y \phantom{{}+ 4z} &= 2 \\ -2y + z &= 3 \end{aligned}
\qquad\longrightarrow\qquad
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & -3 & 4 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & -2 & 1 & 3 \end{array}\right]
$$

The second equation has no $z$, so its third entry is $0$; the third equation has no $x$, so its first entry is $0$.

</Example>

<Example title="Four variables, two equations">

$$
\begin{aligned} x_1 + x_2 - x_3 + 5x_4 &= 1 \\ 3x_1 + x_3 - 2x_4 &= 0 \end{aligned}
\qquad\longrightarrow\qquad
\left[\begin{array}{cccc|c} 1 & 1 & -1 & 5 & 1 \\ 3 & 0 & 1 & -2 & 0 \end{array}\right]
$$

</Example>

## And back again

Reading a matrix as a system is the same rule in reverse: column $j$ holds the coefficients of $x_j$, and the last column holds the constants.

<Example title="From a matrix to its system">

$$
\left[\begin{array}{ccccc|c} 1 & 2 & 0 & 3 & 5 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 1 & -1 & 5 & 6 & 4 \end{array}\right]
\qquad\longrightarrow\qquad
\begin{aligned} x_1 + 2x_2 + 3x_4 + 5x_5 &= 1 \\ x_1 + x_2 + 2x_4 + x_5 &= 0 \\ 3x_1 + x_2 - x_3 + 5x_4 + 6x_5 &= 4 \end{aligned}
$$

</Example>
