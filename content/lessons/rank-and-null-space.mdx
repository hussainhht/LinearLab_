Row reducing a matrix reveals more than the solutions of one system. The pivots describe two sets of vectors attached to every matrix.

## Rank

<Definition term="Rank">

The **rank** of a matrix $A$ is the number of pivots in its reduced row echelon form. It counts the independent columns of $A$ (and, equally, its independent rows).

</Definition>

## Column space

The **column space** of $A$ is the set of all combinations of its columns, which is every vector $A\mathbf{x}$ can produce. So $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ is consistent exactly when $\mathbf{b}$ lies in the column space.

A **basis** for the column space is given by the **pivot columns of the original matrix** $A$. The pivot positions come from the RREF, but the vectors must come from $A$: row operations keep track of which columns are independent while changing the columns themselves.

## Null space

The **null space** of $A$ is the set of all solutions of the homogeneous system $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$. To find a basis, reduce $A$, then for each free variable in turn set it to $1$ and the other free variables to $0$; the pivot variables are fixed by the RREF rows. Each choice gives one basis vector, so the number of basis vectors, the **nullity**, equals the number of free variables.

<Definition term="Rank–nullity theorem">

For an $m \times n$ matrix, every column is either a pivot column or a free column, so

$$
\operatorname{rank} A + \operatorname{nullity} A = n.
$$

</Definition>

<Example title="A 3×4 matrix of rank 2">

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 1 & 4 \\ 3 & 6 & 1 & 7 \end{bmatrix}
\quad\xrightarrow{\text{RREF}}\quad
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
$$

The pivots are in columns 1 and 3, so $\operatorname{rank} A = 2$.

**Column space basis:** columns 1 and 3 of the original $A$,
$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}.$$

**Null space basis:** the free variables are $x_2$ and $x_4$. The RREF rows say $x_1 = -2x_2 - 3x_4$ and $x_3 = 2x_4$.

- $x_2 = 1, x_4 = 0$ gives $(-2, 1, 0, 0)$.
- $x_2 = 0, x_4 = 1$ gives $(-3, 0, 2, 1)$.

So the nullity is 2 and $2 + 2 = 4$ columns, as rank–nullity requires. **Check** the second vector: $A(-3, 0, 2, 1) = (-3 + 0 + 0 + 3,\ -6 + 0 + 2 + 4,\ -9 + 0 + 2 + 7) = (0, 0, 0)$.

</Example>
