If $A$ and $B$ are invertible $n \times n$ matrices and $k \neq 0$, then:

| Rule | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | $I^{-1} = I$ |
| Inverse of an inverse | $(A^{-1})^{-1} = A$ |
| Scalar multiple | $(kA)^{-1} = \frac1k A^{-1}$ |
| Product (order reverses) | $(AB)^{-1} = B^{-1}A^{-1}$ |
| Transpose | $(A^T)^{-1} = (A^{-1})^T$ |
| Powers | $(A^m)^{-1} = (A^{-1})^m$ |

Each rule is proved by multiplying and getting $I$. For the product rule:

$$
(AB)(B^{-1}A^{-1}) = A(BB^{-1})A^{-1} = AIA^{-1} = AA^{-1} = I,
$$

and similarly $(B^{-1}A^{-1})(AB) = I$. Undoing "first $B$, then $A$" means undoing $A$ first, which is why the order reverses.

A **diagonal** matrix with nonzero diagonal entries inverts entry by entry: $\operatorname{diag}(d_1, \dots, d_n)^{-1} = \operatorname{diag}(\frac{1}{d_1}, \dots, \frac{1}{d_n})$. If any $d_i = 0$ it has no inverse.

<Example title="Recovering A from (3A)⁻¹ = [2 −1; 1 1]">

Invert both sides: $3A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}^{-1}$. Here $\Delta = 2 \cdot 1 - (-1) \cdot 1 = 3$, so

$$
3A = \frac13\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
A = \frac19\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac19 & \frac19 \\ -\frac19 & \frac29 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

**Check:** $3A = \begin{bmatrix} \frac13 & \frac13 \\ -\frac13 & \frac23 \end{bmatrix}$, and $\begin{bmatrix} \frac13 & \frac13 \\ -\frac13 & \frac23 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

</Example>

<Example title="Recovering A from (3A)⁻¹ = [−1 5; 1 −2]">

$\Delta = (-1)(-2) - 5 \cdot 1 = -3$, so

$$
3A = -\frac13\begin{bmatrix} -2 & -5 \\ -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac23 & \frac53 \\ \frac13 & \frac13 \end{bmatrix}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
A = \begin{bmatrix} \frac29 & \frac59 \\ \frac19 & \frac19 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

</Example>
