## Row operations and determinants

Each elementary row operation changes the determinant in a predictable way:

| Operation | Effect on det |
| --- | --- |
| Swap two rows | Changes the sign |
| Multiply a row by $k$ | Multiplies det by $k$ |
| Add a multiple of one row to another | No change |

Two consequences: a matrix with a **row of zeros** has determinant 0, and so does a matrix with **two equal rows** (swapping them changes the sign but not the matrix, so $\det A = -\det A$).

## Triangular matrices and an efficient method

The determinant of a triangular matrix is the **product of its diagonal entries**. Combined with the table above, this gives the practical way to compute determinants of any size: reduce to triangular form with replacements and swaps, then multiply the diagonal and fix the sign.

<Example title="det of [1 2 3; 0 4 5; 1 0 6] by elimination">

$$
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 4 & 5 \\ 1 & 0 & 6 \end{bmatrix}
\xrightarrow{R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - R_1}
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 4 & 5 \\ 0 & -2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}
\xrightarrow{R_3 \leftarrow R_3 + \frac12 R_2}
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 4 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{11}{2} \end{bmatrix}
$$

Only replacements were used, so the determinant did not change: $\det A = 1 \cdot 4 \cdot \frac{11}{2} = 22$, matching the cofactor expansion.

</Example>

Because the computation is exact, LinearLab never has to guess whether a determinant is "close enough" to zero. A matrix such as $\begin{bmatrix} 0.000001 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.000001 \end{bmatrix}$ has determinant $10^{-12}$, which is small but not zero, so the matrix is invertible.

## Products, transposes, scalars, inverses

For $n \times n$ matrices:

| Property | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Product | $\det(AB) = \det A \cdot \det B$ |
| Transpose | $\det(A^T) = \det A$ |
| Scalar multiple | $\det(kA) = k^n \det A$ (each of the $n$ rows is scaled) |
| Inverse | $\det(A^{-1}) = \dfrac{1}{\det A}$, from $\det A \cdot \det A^{-1} = \det I = 1$ |

There is **no** simple rule for $\det(A + B)$.

## What the number means

For a 2×2 matrix, $|\det A|$ is the factor by which $A$ scales areas: the unit square is sent to a parallelogram of area $|\det A|$. For 3×3 matrices it scales volumes. The sign records whether orientation is preserved (positive) or flipped (negative). A determinant of 0 means space is flattened onto a line or plane, which is exactly why such a matrix cannot be undone.
