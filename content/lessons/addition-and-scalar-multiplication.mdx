## Addition and subtraction

Matrices of the **same size** are added or subtracted entry by entry:

$$
(A \pm B)_{ij} = a_{ij} \pm b_{ij}.
$$

If the sizes differ, $A + B$ is not defined. There is no way to pad a smaller matrix with zeros.

<Example title="Adding and subtracting 2×2 matrices">

$$
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} 1+5 & 2+6 \\ 3+7 & 4+8 \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 8 \\ 10 & 12 \end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} 1-5 & 2-6 \\ 3-7 & 4-8 \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} -4 & -4 \\ -4 & -4 \end{bmatrix}
$$

</Example>

## Scalar multiplication

A number is called a **scalar** here, to distinguish it from a matrix. Multiplying a matrix by a scalar $k$ multiplies every entry:

$$
(kA)_{ij} = k\,a_{ij}, \qquad
3\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 6 \\ 9 & 12 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

Subtraction is addition of a scalar multiple: $A - B = A + (-1)B$.

## Properties

These rules hold whenever the sizes allow the operations. They match the rules for ordinary numbers, which is what makes matrix algebra feel familiar.

| Rule | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Commutative | $A + B = B + A$ |
| Associative | $(A + B) + C = A + (B + C)$ |
| Zero matrix | $A + 0 = A$ |
| Additive inverse | $A + (-A) = 0$ |
| Scalars 1 and 0 | $1A = A$ and $0A = 0$ |
| Distributive over matrices | $k(A + B) = kA + kB$ |
| Distributive over scalars | $(k + m)A = kA + mA$ |
| Associative for scalars | $k(mA) = (km)A$ |

Subtraction, like subtraction of numbers, is not commutative: $B - A = -(A - B)$.
